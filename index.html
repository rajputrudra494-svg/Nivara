What is Nivara Bioform

A plastic alternative grown, not drilled.

Nivara Bioform is made of PHA (polyhydroxyalkanoate): a natural polyester that microbes make and store inside their cells as an energy reserve, much like we store fat.

We feed microbes waste streams such as used cooking oil and plant residues, harvest the polymer they build up, and compound it into pellets that run on the same moulding lines as conventional plastic. Because nature made the molecule, microbes in soil, compost and seawater recognise it and can digest it back into CO₂, water and biomass.

How it's made

01 / 05 · Feed

Start with waste.

Used cooking oil, plant residues and other second-generation feedstocks become the food source. Nothing is taken from the food chain or the ground.

02 / 05 · Ferment

Microbes do the work.

In fermentation tanks, microbes eat the feedstock and store the surplus as PHA granules inside their cells. Each cell becomes a tiny polymer factory.

03 / 05 · Harvest

Harvest & formulate.

The polymer is extracted, purified and compounded into Nivara Bioform pellets, tuned for stiffness, clarity, barrier and colour.

04 / 05 · Form

Shape it like plastic.

Converters mould bottles, jars, caps, trays and films on the machines they already own. No new factory required.

05 / 05 · Return

Give it back.

After use, microbes in compost, soil or the sea digest the material into CO₂, water and biomass, closing the loop where it began.

How it stacks up

Not all "bioplastics" are equal.

Many plant-based plastics only break down in industrial composters. Nivara Bioform is designed to break down where waste actually ends up.
